Understanding Unigrams, Bigrams & N-Grams

Core theoretical concepts in Natural Language Processing and probabilistic language models.

N=1

Unigram Model

Considers each word independently without context. Assumes occurrence of a word does not depend on preceding words (Bag-of-Words assumption).

P(w₁, w₂, w₃) = P(w₁) × P(w₂) × P(w₃)
N=2

Bigram Model

Considers pairs of consecutive words. Employs a first-order Markov assumption where probability of a word depends only on its immediately previous word.

P(wᵢ | wᵢ₋₁) = Count(wᵢ₋₁, wᵢ) / Count(wᵢ₋₁)
N=k

General N-Gram

Extends context to N-1 previous history tokens. Higher N captures richer context but suffers from data sparsity if corpus is small.

P(wᵢ | wᵢ₋ₙ₊₁...wᵢ₋₁)

Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) Formula

In an N-gram language model, the conditional probability of target token $w_n$ given history $w_1 \dots w_{n-1}$ is calculated by dividing frequency counts:

P_MLE( w_i | w_{i-N+1} \dots w_{i-1} ) = Count( w_{i-N+1} \dots w_i ) / Count( w_{i-N+1} \dots w_{i-1} )
